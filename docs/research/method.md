# Research method

## Evidence standard

For each material claim, record the exact URL, page or artifact, date checked, and whether it is a brand claim, third-party report, customer review, or our interpretation. Prefer primary sources for product specifications and policies. Use independent sources for market and customer context.

## A practical pass

1. **Discover:** collect candidate brands from search, retail shelves, marketplaces, customer conversations, and adjacent categories.
2. **Qualify:** confirm that a current plain white tee is available and classify the brand as direct, adjacent, or reference.
3. **Capture:** complete one profile using the same fields and record prices with currency, market, and date.
4. **Compare:** look for repeated promises, product trade-offs, pricing structures, and points of friction.
5. **Validate:** check promising patterns against more than one independent source or direct customer research.

## Fact versus inference

- **Fact:** directly supported by an identifiable source, such as a listed fiber composition.
- **Brand claim:** language or assertion made by the company; do not present it as independently verified.
- **Customer signal:** an individual review or comment; useful as a clue, not proof of prevalence.
- **Inference:** our interpretation of evidence; label it and include confidence or competing explanations.

## Snapshot discipline

Prices, sizes, stock, return terms, and product pages change. Date each check. Save a short excerpt or relevant details in the profile so future readers can understand the observation if the page changes.
