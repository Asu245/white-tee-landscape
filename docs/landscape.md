# Market landscape

## Research question

How do brands make a plain white T-shirt compelling, credible, and distinct, and which customer needs remain underserved?

## Working boundaries

- **Core product:** adult plain white short-sleeve crew-neck T-shirts. Record variations such as heavyweight, undershirt, multipack, women’s cut, or premium basics separately.
- **Geography:** United States for the first pass; note when a product or price is specific to another market.
- **Market stage:** research only. No brand, product, or supplier decisions have been made.
- **Competitor set:** to be validated. A brand can be direct, adjacent, or a reference point.

## Questions to answer

1. Which customer and use case does each brand target?
2. What tangible product choices support its promise: fabric, weight, fit, construction, finish, and care?
3. How does it price one shirt versus a bundle, and what is included in that price?
4. What proof does it offer for quality, sourcing, durability, or sustainability claims?
5. How does it acquire customers and reduce purchase risk (reviews, guarantees, returns, sizing help)?
6. Which needs or trade-offs show up repeatedly in customer feedback?

## Comparison dimensions

| Dimension | Capture |
|---|---|
| Customer and use | Audience, occasion, wardrobe role |
| Product | Fit, size range, fabric/fiber, weight, construction, opacity, shrinkage, care |
| Offer | Single price, multipack price, promotions, shipping, returns, guarantee |
| Positioning | Main promise, tone, visual identity, proof points |
| Distribution | Direct-to-consumer, retail, wholesale, marketplaces, geography |
| Experience | Product page, sizing guidance, reviews, packaging, post-purchase support |
| Business signals | Assortment breadth, launch cadence, retail footprint; only when sourced |
